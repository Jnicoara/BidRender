# Pack label scan — whole pricing sheet (2026-10-13, Track C)

**Read-only. Nothing is changed.** This scan covers every row of
`starter-catalog-pricing.xlsx` ("Starter prices", 1,717 rows, the app-order
sort of `28245633`). The workbook was read from a fresh copy, because the
owner has it open. It looks for rows whose pack qty is **1** (`per foot`,
`per foot (…)` or `each`) while the item is actually sold on a **reel, coil,
roll, length or box**. All of them are listed here, so A can relabel them in
one pass (`packFor` and the seed's pack).

**Why it matters.** "Price per unit" is "Pack price ÷ Pack qty". If the pack
qty is 1 and the owner types what the counter or the shelf tag says (a reel,
a box, a 10 ft length), the whole pack is priced as one foot or one piece. A
starter that uses 2 of them, or a run type that uses 200 ft, multiplies the
mistake.

**The search, stated so the gap is visible.** 1,291 rows have pack qty 1:
1,145 `each`, 73 `per foot (cut to length)`, 31 `per foot (from 250 ft
roll)`, 21 `per foot (from 1000 ft box)`, 7 `per foot (from 500 ft spool)`
and 14 plain `per foot`.

- **Read in full:** all 14 plain `per foot` rows, and all 59 rows that state a
  roll, spool or box length.
- **The 1,145 `each` rows were filtered by name**, by category and by words
  that usually mean a pack or a length (tape, strap, clip, terminal, splice,
  label, tube, wire, rail, channel, bolt, hanger, grommet, and others), and
  every hit was read.
- **Not checked one by one:** the 73 `cut to length` rows (wire sold off the
  reel by the foot, which is the label's point), and `each` rows whose names
  matched none of those words: devices, boxes, panels, breakers, fixtures and
  equipment. They are normally priced one at a time, but a `box of 10` device
  could still be hiding there. A recount by A should search the same way and
  say what it searched for.

**Two conventions already ship, and both are right for their kind.** Rows
below say which one each fits.

- **Per-foot items on a reel:** pack qty stays **1**. The label names the
  reel, "per foot (from 250 ft roll)", and the owner types the PER-FOOT price.
  That is the owner's wire rule, and it is what the 59 labelled wire rows do.
- **Rigid lengths and boxed pieces:** the pack is the thing bought, for
  example `10 ft stick` / `10` (all conduit) or `box of 25` / `25`. The owner
  types the PACK price.

**Listings** were checked on 2026-10-13 at platt.com and homedepot.com, with
another supply house named where neither showed the pack. Prices only show
how large a mistake would be; they are not proposed prices.

## E. Every row whose pack came from a WORD in its name (2026-10-14)

**Owner, 2026-10-14:** list every row on the sheet where the keyword pack rule
set a pack size, and mark the ones that already have a price typed in column
I. This section is that list, for A's relabel pass. It is the opposite fault
to sections A–D: there, a pack of 1 hid a reel or a box; here a box pack was
**guessed from a word**, and sometimes the word was in the name by accident.

**How the pack gets there.** `pricing/buildStarterSheets.mts` writes
`packFor(m)` into every row (`pricing/starterSheetLayout.ts` line 420), then
carries over anything the owner typed. `packFor` guesses from words:

- line 448: "wire nut", "push-in connector", "staple", "screw", "anchor",
  "washer", "nut", "zip tie", "ferrule", "clip", "pin" → **box of 100**;
- line 454: a 15A or 20A receptacle or switch (not GFCI, AFCI, dual, smart,
  USB) → **box of 10**;
- line 459: "wall plate", "blank plate", "mud ring", "plaster ring",
  "knockout", "bushing", "connector", "coupling", "strap", "clamp", "nail
  plate" → **box of 25**;
- line 434, per-foot rows: "EMT", "rigid", "PVC", "IMC", "flex", "FMC",
  "liquidtight" → **10 ft stick** / 10.

The words are matched ANYWHERE in the name, so "Dish**washer** whip" is a box
of 100 washers and "**Screw**less wall plate" a box of 100 screws.

**What was searched, so the gap is visible.** Every shipped row
(`BASELINE_MATERIALS`, read 2026-10-14) was run through the same three word
rules in the same order, and each hit was checked against `packFor`'s own
answer (all agreed), then matched by name to a fresh copy of the sheet for
its row, its current pack, column I and "Used by". Per-foot rows whose label
also comes from words (`per foot (from 250 ft roll)` and the rest, 146 rows)
are **not listed**: their pack qty is always 1 by the owner's 2026-10-12
rule, so a wrong word changes a label, never a price. The 47 per-foot rows the
words set to `10 ft stick` / 10 ARE listed (table E2), because there the
qty divides the price.

**Counts:** **380 rows** got a box pack from a word (84 box of 100, 18 box of
10, 278 box of 25), and **47 rows** a 10 ft stick. **Only 4 have a price
typed in column I**, and the owner had already overwritten the guessed pack
on all four: row 752 (`each`, $2.14) and the flex rows 154, 155, 160 (`100
ft coil`, $213 / $274 / $124). So no typed price today sits on a guessed
pack — which is why this pass has to happen **before** more prices are
typed.

**How the notes were made.** A row is marked **ACCIDENTAL** only where the
word plainly is not the product (7 rows). "Likely accidental" and "Check"
rows (the 2-1/2"–4" fittings and the large splices among them) are for A to
confirm against one listing. A note naming an earlier review means that
review already said what the pack is. A blank note means the guess looks
right and nobody has checked it against a listing; it is still a guess.
