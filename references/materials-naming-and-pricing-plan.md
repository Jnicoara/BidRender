# Materials — naming, then pricing once. PLAN ONLY, 2026-10-01

**Status: nothing here is built.** No seed file, no migration, no script is
changed by this document. Measured against `local-dev` at `659782a`.

**The goal, in the owner's words:** "nail down the materials list so I can
price it once."

**The decisions this plan carries out (the owner's, 2026-10-01, not to be
reopened):**

- Wire and cable: size and conductors, then type, then the metal spelled out
  at the END — "6/3 SER Copper", "1/0-3 SER Aluminum" (a dash in place of the
  slash when the size itself has a slash). Every wire and cable row ends in
  Copper or Aluminum. Old forms become search aliases.
- Commodity items: no brand, no metal suffix. Brands on panels and breakers
  only.
- Sort: category, then type, then size, with the app's size table.
- "1-Pole" everywhere. **Confirmed 2026-10-05 for BREAKERS only** (§ 8 Q1);
  a single-pole switch keeps its name.

> **READ FIRST — three of these REVERSE decisions the repo has recorded and
> tests that enforce them.** Carrying them out is fine; doing it without
> saying so is the failure CLAUDE.md § "Where decisions live" describes.
>
> 1. **"1-Pole" reverses "Single-Pole".** CLAUDE.md § Brands, "One
>    convention for single-pole breakers: Single-Pole — DONE 2026-09-24",
>    chose "Single-Pole" over "1-Pole" on purpose ("that is how a one-pole
>    breaker is said and written"), and `ASSEMBLIES_PLAN.md` step 6 says the
>    same. `server/materialsCatalog.test.ts` "writes pole count the way a
>    supply house does" pins it. **If confirmed, CLAUDE.md and
>    ASSEMBLIES_PLAN.md step 6 change in the SAME commit as the rename**,
>    each saying what replaced it and when. Open question 1.
> 2. **Metal spelled out reverses "AL / CU".** Decided 2026-09-25
>    (`server/seed/materials/wireAndCable.ts` header; "writes a stated metal
>    as AL / CU" in the test file). `shared/materialSearchRank.ts`
>    `withMetalWords` exists only because of that decision.
> 3. **"6/3 SER" reverses "every SER row by its full conductor set".**
>    Decided 2026-09-25 (`wireAndCable.ts` § "Every SER row is named by its
>    FULL conductor set"; test "names every SER cable by its full conductor
>    set, never '-3' shorthand"). The recorded reason: **the shorthand hides
>    the ground size**, and it once let "4/0-3 SER aluminum" ship beside
>    "4/0-4/0-4/0-2/0 SER AL" as two rows for one cable. § 1.3 keeps that
>    protection under the new names. Open question 2.

### 1.3 Where the new wire rule needs care — measured

1. **The app cannot read "14/2" as a size today.** Measured with the app's
   own functions on the proposed names:
   - `materialTypeName("14/2 NM-B Copper")` is **null**, so it groups with
     nothing. "1/0-3 SER Aluminum" does group ("SER Aluminum").
   - `compareBySize` sorts **10/2, 12/2, 14/2 as 10, 12 and 14 AMPS**,
     because "/2" is the old "20/2 breaker" amperage rule. That is the wrong
     gauge order, and it puts cable among the amperage-sized rows.
   - **`mcFittingNames("12/2 MC cable Copper")` returns null** (it matches
     `/^(\d+)-(\d) MC cable\b/`). So **every MC cable run would silently
     lose its connectors and straps on the bid**: a smaller number with
     nothing on screen to say so.

   **So the parser change ships FIRST, accepting both the dash and the slash
   form, with tests**, before any row is renamed (§ 7, piece 2). Low-voltage
   "18/2 control wire" and "16/2 speaker wire" are ALREADY in the slash form
   and already ungrouped today (`materialTypeName` gives null): the same fix
   repairs them.

2. **"3/4 MC cable Copper" reads as three-quarter inch.** A #3,
   four-conductor MC cable becomes "3/4", the trade size every estimator
   types for 3/4" EMT. "1/3", "2/3" and "4/3" read as fractions too. Search
   will cross the streams ("3/4" finds both). Open question 2.
3. **SER and the ground size.** "6/3 SER Copper" drops the ground size, the
   number the full set was chosen to show. To keep the protection:
   - the full set goes in the **description** ("Three #6 insulated and a #6
     ground — 6-6-6-6") and stays an **alias**, so "6-6-6-6" still finds it;
   - a test fails if two SER rows share a size + metal, which is exactly how
     the "4/0-3" duplicate shipped;
   - **the two THREE-conductor sets have no "-3" form.** "4/0-4/0-2/0" (two
     hots and a reduced neutral) and "250-250-250" are not "4/0-3" and
     "250-3". Calling them that would reproduce the duplicate. **Proposed:
     they keep the full set and only gain the metal word.** Open question 2.
   - **"6-6-6-8" as an alias, as the decision lists, is a DIFFERENT cable**
     (#8 ground) from the shipped "6-6-6-6". CLAUDE.md: "never alias one
     material to a different material". Either add a 6-6-6-8 row or leave
     that alias off. Open question 2.
4. **The alias rules still hold, with three clean-ups in the same commit:**
   - drop the alias words the new names now contain ("copper", "aluminum",
     "14/2", "6/3" on the SER rows), or "never restates a word the
     material's own name already contains" goes red;
   - the old names become aliases of their OWN row only (no other row
     carries them, so "never aliases one material to the full name of a
     different one" holds);
   - `withMetalWords` in `shared/materialSearchRank.ts` becomes dead and is
     removed.

   **A found fault worth fixing in the same pass:** `gaugeAliases` turns
   "#1/0" into "10ga" and "#2/0" into "20ga", so a search for "10ga" can
   rank 1/0 wire. A search ranking fault, not a price.

## 2. SAFE RENAME

**What a rename must not touch, and why it does not:**

| Thing                 | Points at the material by…                         | Effect of an in-place rename                                                                                                                         |
| --------------------- | -------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| An old bid line       | its own `name` + frozen `snapshot*` price fields   | **None.** The line keeps the name and price it was added with.                                                                                       |
| An assembly part      | `assembly_materials.materialId`                    | None: same id.                                                                                                                                       |
| A run type, a takeoff | material ids                                       | None: same id.                                                                                                                                       |
| A contractor's fork   | its own row (`userId` set)                         | **None.** `renameBaselineMaterials` is scoped `isNull(materials.userId)`. The fork keeps its old name; `importPrices` matches it by that name first. |
| The seed itself       | **NAME** — every boot matches shipped rows by name | **This is the trap**, and the rename map is what defuses it.                                                                                         |

**Why a plain text edit is wrong:** the seeder would not find "12-2 NM-B",
insert "12/2 NM-B Copper" as a NEW row, and leave the old one orphaned with
every reference still on it. Two rows, one name each, and nobody can tell
which is current.

### The steps, in one commit (stop `pnpm dev` first, as CLAUDE.md says)

1. **Parser first, in an EARLIER commit (§ 1.3 item 1).** `readSize`,
   `SIZE_PREFIXES` and `mcFittingNames` learn the slash form while still
   reading the dash form. Tests prove both, including that an MC run on
   "12/2 MC cable Copper" still gets its connector and straps. This ships on
   its own, so the rename never meets a parser that cannot read it.
2. **Seed names** in `server/seed/materials/wireAndCable.ts` (and
   `power.ts` for poles) change to the new names.
3. **`RENAMED_BASELINE_MATERIALS`** (`shared/renamedMaterials.ts`) gets
   old → new for every row in § 1.1. **Every OLDER spelling also points
   straight at the final name**: "6-3 SER copper", "6-3 SER CU" and
   "6-6-6-6 SER CU" all → "6/3 SER Copper"; "#8 XHHW aluminum" and
   "#8 XHHW AL" → "#8 XHHW Aluminum". The existing entries that point at the
   AL/CU names are **edited** to point at the new ones, so no database
   depends on the rename pass walking a chain in order (the rule the file
   already states).
4. **Aliases:** each old name becomes an alias of its own row, with the
   clean-ups in § 1.3 item 4.
5. **Every place that names a material by EXACT name changes in the same
   commit.** Each one silently stops resolving otherwise:
   - `server/seed/baselineAssemblies.ts`: "12-2 NM-B" ×3, "14-2 NM-B" ×4,
     "#8 THHN" ×1. CLAUDE.md: starter assemblies match by exact name and
     IGNORE the rename map;
   - `server/seed/baselineRunTypes.ts`: "#12 THHN" ×2, "12-2 MC cable" ×2,
     "12-3 MC cable" ×2;
   - `shared/materialCommonness.ts`: a name-keyed table ("14-2 NM-B": core
     …). A missed key is silently ranked lower in search;
   - `shared/runRespecify.ts`, `shared/runTypeLabor.ts` (comments) and
     `client/src/components/takeoff/RunsPanel.tsx` (a literal);
   - **25 test files** name these materials. They change with the names.
6. **Decision records:** the `wireAndCable.ts` header, CLAUDE.md § Brands
   (poles), and `ASSEMBLIES_PLAN.md` step 6 each say what replaced them and
   when.
7. **Tests that now fail on purpose are rewritten**, not deleted: "writes a
   stated metal as AL / CU" becomes "spells the metal out at the end"; the
   full-set SER test becomes "no two SER rows share size + metal"; the pole
   test changes words.
8. **Check before merging:** `pnpm check`, the full suite,
   `pnpm tsx scripts/searchSpotCheck.mts` before AND after (the common
   failure is a right row ranked fourth), and `scripts/dropOrphanBaselines.mts`
   on a local copy after one boot, expecting 0 orphans. **If it reports any,
   stop and find out why.**

**Migration: none.** A rename in place is a seed change, reaching every
database on its next boot.

## 3. THE BOOT PROBLEM

**Confirmed on `local-dev` (`server/db.ts`, `backfillMaterialMetadata`):**
on every start it reads every row with `userId IS NULL` and, where
`Number(row.costPerUnit) !== Number(seed.costPerUnit)`, writes the seed
value back. Today every seed price is `UNPRICED` (0), so **any price on a
shipped row is reset to $0 on the next boot.**
`server/materialsCatalog.test.ts` "drags every shipped row back to unpriced"
pins it. A contractor's own price is safe: forks are untouched
(`seedPreservesUserPrices.test.ts`).

**That is the design, not the bug.** CLAUDE.md § "Where a priced catalog
lands" already decided it: the seed file is the source of truth for every
shipped price, and nothing types a price onto a baseline row. **The fix is
to put the prices IN the seed**, which the same re-stamp then delivers to
every database on the next deploy.

### The fix

1. **A generated price file** beside the seed, for example
   `server/seed/materials/examplePrices.generated.ts`, keyed by catalog
   name: `costPerUnit`, `packQty`, `packPrice`, source, and as-of date. It is
   written by the loader (§ 5), never by hand. The seed modules read
   `costPerUnit` from it in place of `UNPRICED`. A name in the price file
   that is not in the catalog **fails the build** (a test), so a rename can
   never strand a price.
2. **The "example price" label — BEFORE any price is non-zero.** Once
   shipped rows carry prices, `costPerUnit === 0` stops meaning "nobody
   priced this", and the Materials screen would report a priced catalog no
   contractor has checked. Already planned, and it must land first:
   `migrations-0098-batch-plan.md` § 10b, owner answers B2/B3, and § R group
   E (on `a-migrations-plan`):
   - `materials.examplePriceSource varchar(128) NULL` and
     `materials.examplePriceAsOf date NULL`. "Is this an example?" is ONE
     fact, `examplePriceSource IS NOT NULL`;
   - **the label reads "BidRidge example · <date>"** (owner B3: not a
     supplier name);
   - **editing the price clears both** in the same update, which is what
     makes it the contractor's own;
   - `bid_line_items.snapshotPriceWasExample boolean NULL`, frozen with the
     other snapshots, so a quote can say which lines went out on example
     prices.
3. **The order, and it is not optional:** group E's columns (migration, step
   1. → code that reads `examplePriceSource` as the new "not yours yet"
      signal (`shared/materialPricing.ts`, the unpriced filter, the label) →
      only then the seed with prices. **Never the prices first.**
4. **Tests that change:** "ships every row unpriced" becomes "every priced
   shipped row carries a source and a date"; "drags every shipped row back to
   unpriced" becomes "back to the seed price". **The re-stamp itself stays.**

## 5. PRICING WORKFLOW

### 5.1 Regenerate the sheet with the new names, keeping typed prices

**Measured: the committed `pricing/starter-catalog-pricing.xlsx` has 0
pack prices typed** (Generic 1,678 rows, Brand variants 519 rows; "Pack
qty" is filled on all of them by the generator). If the owner's working copy
has prices, they live in that copy, and the regenerator must carry them:

1. `pricing/buildPricingSheet.mts` (unchanged inputs: the real catalog plus
   its job-type additions) writes `rows.json`.
2. **New: a carry-over step** reads the owner's existing workbook and copies
   **Pack price** (and an edited Pack qty) onto the new sheet:
   - **by name**;
   - **then through `RENAMED_BASELINE_MATERIALS`**, so a price typed on
     "12-2 NM-B" lands on "12/2 NM-B Copper";
   - **then through `pricing/movedFromSheet.ts`** (the sheet's own renames).
   - Any typed price that matches nothing is **listed, never dropped**:
     "3 typed prices have no row in the new sheet: …".
3. `pricing/writeWorkbook.cjs` writes the workbook. exceljs stays out of
   `package.json`, as the file says.

### 5.2 The round trip

1. **The owner fills Pack price only.** Price per unit is a formula.
2. **`pricing/validatePrices.mts` (new)** reads the workbook and checks every
   row:
   - **blank** pack price: listed, a warning. A blank row stays unpriced, $0,
     flagged, which is honest;
   - **not a number**, zero, negative, or more than 2 decimals: an error;
   - **pack sanity:** Pack qty is a whole number ≥ 1; a "foot" row's pack is
     feet (a 500 ft or 1000 ft reel, not "1"); an "each" row's pack is
     pieces. A mismatch is an error;
   - **unit sanity:** the per-unit price lies inside a broad band for its
     category and unit (wire per foot, breakers each, and so on). Outside:
     an error;
   - **outliers inside a family:** in one type (§ 1.5), sorted by the size
     table, a bigger size should not cost less than a smaller one, and a step
     more than 3× its neighbour is a warning. Aluminum below copper at the
     same size, or it warns;
   - **brand variants:** within a band of the other brands under the same
     parent, or a warning;
   - **names:** every sheet name must be a catalog name (after the rename
     map). An unknown name is an error.
3. **What a failure looks like.** It exits 1, writes NOTHING, and prints one
   line per problem, with the sheet row, so it can be fixed in Excel:

   ```
   validatePrices: 3 errors, 2 warnings — nothing was written.
   ERROR  row 412  #10 THHN Copper      $0.02/ft is below #12 THHN Copper ($0.11/ft): a bigger wire cannot cost less.
   ERROR  row 87   1/2" EMT             Pack qty "1" on a 10 ft stick sold by the foot: pack should be 10.
   ERROR  row 1033 20A 1-Pole brekaer   not in the catalog (typo, or a renamed row?).
   WARN   row 1501 #2 XHHW Aluminum     $1.90/ft is above #2 THHN Copper ($1.40/ft).
   WARN   row 640  4" EMT coupling      $14.20 is 4.1x the 3-1/2" one.
   ```

   **Errors always block.** A warning blocks until it is accepted by name in
   `pricing/accepted-warnings.txt`, so an outlier nobody looked at cannot
   slip in, and one that is genuinely right is accepted once.

4. **Load:** with no errors and all warnings accepted, the same script writes
   `examplePrices.generated.ts` (§ 3) with today's date and the source
   "BidRidge example". The commit is the seed change, and the deploy re-stamps
   every database.
5. **"A test bid with known totals still matches":**
   - **The known-answer bid** (`build-pipeline-plan.md` § 5) uses FIXTURE
     prices, as CLAUDE.md requires. So it must stay green unchanged, and it
     proves loading prices moved no arithmetic.
   - **A separate, non-test report**, `pricing/starterTotals.mts`, prices the
     8 starter assemblies and one sample bid from the newly loaded seed and
     prints the totals for the owner to eyeball before committing. A test
     cannot assert those, because a test must not borrow shipped prices.

## 6. IMPORT FOR REAL SUPPLIER FILES — after the starter prices

**Today (`materialsRouter.importPrices`):**

- takes rows of name + cost, up to 5,000;
- matches by exact name, then through the rename map; **not by alias**;
- **writes as it goes**: each match forks a shipped row and stamps the price
  immediately;
- returns priced / unmatched / renamed lists;
- **no preview, no review screen, no undo.** A wrong column, or a
  case-per-foot mix-up, re-prices hundreds of rows at once, and the only way
  back is by hand.

**What the review screen needs:**

1. **A dry run**: the same matcher, writing nothing. It returns per row the
   material, the old price, the new price, the % change, and flags: unit
   mismatch, a change over ±50%, matched only through a rename.
2. **Unmatched rows with suggestions** from the existing search, and a pick
   to pair them by hand. Never auto-paired.
3. **Apply selected** rows only.
4. **Undo the import.** That needs a record of what it changed, so it is a
   **migration (flag for the numbered list in § R, do not number here)**:
   - an import batch table (id, owner, supplier, created, row count);
   - a per-row table: material id, previous price, new price.
   - "Undo" restores the previous price **only where the row has not been
     edited since**, and lists the rest.
5. **Bid lines are untouched either way**: they hold snapshots.
